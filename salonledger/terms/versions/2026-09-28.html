1. Accepting These Terms

These Terms of Use are an agreement between NeuEra Apps LLC and the salon owner who uses Salon Ledger — and, where the owner signs up on behalf of a business, that business. In these terms, "you" means the owner, and "techs" means the people the owner invites to log tickets.

By signing in and setting up a salon, you accept these terms. If you do not accept them, do not use Salon Ledger.

Techs use Salon Ledger at the owner's invitation and do not enter into a separate agreement with us, but anyone who uses a linked phone must follow Acceptable Use.

Our Privacy Policy explains what Salon Ledger records and forms part of this agreement.

2. Who May Use It

To set up a salon you must be at least 18 years old and able to enter into this agreement — and, if you are acting for a business, authorised to accept these terms on its behalf.

Salon Ledger is a business tool. It is for recording the work done in a salon, not for personal use.

3. The Closed Pilot

Salon Ledger is in a closed pilot, open only to salons we have invited. We would rather say so here than let you discover it.

That means the service is provided as it is. Features may change or be removed, availability is not guaranteed, and no service level is promised. We may end the pilot, or your participation in it; when we do, we will try to give you reasonable notice so that you can export your totals first.

There is no fee during the pilot. If Salon Ledger is offered for a fee later, we will tell you what it costs before anything is charged, and nothing will be charged without your agreement.

4. What Salon Ledger Is, and What It Is Not

Salon Ledger replaces the paper service-ticket pad. Techs log tickets on their phones — the services, their prices, and an optional tip marked cash or card — and you see every ticket, correct them, close pay periods, and export each tech's totals as a CSV file.

  • It does not process payments. No money moves through Salon Ledger. A tip on a ticket is a record that a tip was given; the tip itself is paid and handled by you and your techs, outside the app.
  • It is not payroll. It adds up tickets. It does not calculate wages, commission, withholding, overtime or tax, and it does not pay anyone.
  • It is not advice. Totals are a record-keeping aid. They are not payroll, tax, accounting or legal advice, and you should not treat them as such.
  • It does not record your customers. Tickets have no customer name or contact. Do not enter customer details anywhere else in the app either, such as in a service name.

5. Your Account and Your Salon

You sign in with an email address, and each account has one salon. Keep access to that email address secure, and do not share your sign-in with anyone.

Sign-in during the pilot is a simplified mechanism, not yet the production authentication system, as the Privacy Policy explains. Decide what you record with that in mind.

You are responsible for what happens in your salon's account, including on the phones you link to it.

6. Techs and Linked Phones

You add techs by name and link each tech's phone by showing it a one-time QR code. Treat that code like a key: show it only to the tech whose phone you are linking.

You are responsible for having the right to enter your techs' names and to record their work in Salon Ledger, and for telling your techs that you use it and what it records. The Privacy Policy sets out what that is.

If a phone is lost, or a tech stops working with you, unlink the phone. Until you do, that phone can keep logging tickets to your salon.

7. Tickets, Corrections and History

Tickets can be logged without a connection and are sent to Salon Ledger when the phone next connects. A ticket is not in your totals until it has arrived.

Your edits take precedence over a tech's edits. When you correct a ticket, your correction stands. Every change is kept in the ticket's history — what changed, who changed it, and when — whether it was made by you or by a tech.

Tickets are not deleted. A ticket that should not count is voided, which keeps it and its history. A change sent from a phone that Salon Ledger does not accept is kept for you to review rather than discarded.

Salon Ledger records what you and your techs put into it. You are responsible for:

  • the accuracy of the tickets in your salon, and of the totals you rely on;
  • checking totals before you pay anyone from them;
  • how you pay your techs, and the arrangement you have with each of them;
  • your obligations as a business — including employment, wage, tip, tax and record-keeping obligations — which Salon Ledger does not fulfil for you;
  • keeping your own copies of records you are required to keep, for example by exporting them.

  • attempt to break, overload, reverse engineer or gain unauthorised access to the service;
